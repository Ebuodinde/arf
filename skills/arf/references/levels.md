# Levels

Calibrate scope, depth, and claims to the level. Over-claiming at undergraduate level reads as false as under-delivering at doctoral level.

## undergrad (lisans)
- Goal: show understanding of a debate and the ability to build a sourced argument.
- Typical length: 2,500–8,000 words (term paper) or 10,000–15,000 (bitirme tezi).
- Contribution: clear synthesis or a well-defended position; small case study or secondary-data analysis is a plus.
- Sources: 10–25, mostly textbooks, review articles, key papers.
- Register: clear, precise, fewer field-internal abbreviations; define terms.
- Examiner checks: correct citation, no misreading of sources, coherent structure, own voice visible.

## masters (yüksek lisans)
- Goal: command of a literature and a defined, modest original contribution.
- Typical length: articles 6,000–10,000; thesis chapters 8,000–15,000; full thesis 20,000–40,000.
- Contribution: original analysis, a case, a dataset, a method applied to a new context, or a critical synthesis that reorganizes a field.
- Sources: 30–60+, primarily peer-reviewed, recent work included, methodological literature cited.
- Register: field-standard terminology used precisely; explicit methodology; limitations section.
- Examiner checks: gap is real, method fits the question, discussion connects back to literature.

## doctoral (doktora / journal)
- Goal: a contribution reviewers in the field would recognize as new.
- Length per venue (check author guidelines).
- Contribution stated in the introduction in one or two sentences and defended against the closest prior work by name.
- Sources: comprehensive in the niche, includes the most recent 1–2 years, engages directly with competing accounts.
- Register: economical, confident, field-specific; claims scoped exactly.
- Examiner/reviewer checks: novelty, rigor, replicability, positioning, implications.
