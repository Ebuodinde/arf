# Genres

Pick one in the brief; it determines the outline skeleton.

## Empirical article (IMRaD)
Introduction (context → gap → RQ → contribution) · Literature/Background · Method (design, data, sample, instruments, analysis, ethics) · Results (no interpretation) · Discussion (answer RQ, compare with literature, limitations, implications) · Conclusion.
The agent never fabricates data or results. Results come from data the user supplies or analyses actually run; otherwise write a proposal/protocol instead.

## Literature review
Narrative, scoping, or systematic. State the type and follow its conventions (PRISMA for systematic/scoping: search strategy, inclusion/exclusion, flow counts). Organize thematically or by debate, not source by source. End with gaps and an agenda.

## Theoretical / argumentative essay
Thesis up front · conceptual groundwork · argument in steps · strongest objections and replies · implications. Common in humanities, law, philosophy, social theory.

## Case study
Case selection rationale · context · analytical framework · analysis · what generalizes and what does not.

## Thesis chapter
Follow the thesis's overall structure; each chapter opens by linking to the RQ and closes by handing off to the next chapter.

## Research proposal
Problem · literature · RQ/hypotheses · method · timeline · expected contribution · references.

## Discipline notes
- Humanities: longer paragraphs, close reading, quotations with page numbers, first person often acceptable.
- Social sciences: explicit theory and method, APA common.
- STEM/medicine: concise, passive or "we", numbers with units and uncertainty, figures carry results.
- Law: footnote citation (e.g. OSCOLA/Bluebook/ISNAD), statute and case references precise.
Ask the supervisor when the field's convention is unclear.
